01 — The Opportunity

SMBs are buying AI in fragments. The margin is in the consolidation.

A typical small business now runs one subscription for chat, another for slides, another for meeting notes, another for images — each with its own login, its own bill, and no knowledge of the company's documents. Three shifts make one consolidated, white-label workspace viable right now.

A

Open weights crossed the quality line

GLM, DeepSeek, Qwen and GPT-OSS now ship under MIT and Apache licences at frontier-class quality for office work. For the everyday tasks SMBs actually run, the premium for closed models has collapsed to near zero.

B

Inference became a commodity spread

Routine office tasks now clear at $0.06–0.40 per million tokens. Routing each task to the cheapest capable model prices the whole product at ~$0.35 per user per month — roughly a 50× spread against a £29 seat.

C

SMBs want one bill — and their own brand

Each client gets the suite on their own domain, logo and house tone, grounded in their own documents. White-labelling also opens a reseller channel: agencies, MSPs and consultancies selling the platform as theirs.

03 — How It Works

The margin is the router.

Every request flows through a routing gateway that selects the cheapest capable open-weight model for the task, enforces per-tenant budgets, and fails over automatically. Models are configuration, not code — when the market reprices (it does, monthly), the routing table changes, not the product.

04 — Business Model

Priced per seat, because costs scale per seat.

The two largest variable costs — connected email accounts and model usage — grow with headcount, not with tenants. Per-seat pricing absorbs them structurally; the flat-fee competitors quietly bleed on both. A hybrid go-to-market pairs self-serve tiers with a white-glove managed tier.

Fair use without surprise invoices

Soft token caps per seat are enforced at the gateway. When a tenant exceeds them, requests route to the cheapest model tier with an in-app nudge — graceful degradation, never an overage bill. Meeting hours and images carry hard pooled caps with self-serve top-ups.

Meeting intelligence is deliberately gated to Pro: at ~$11 per tenant per month it is the single largest variable cost, and it is exactly where flat-fee competitor economics fail.

Setup fees price in the labour. Managed onboarding (£1,500) covers vault curation, prompt tuning and training — the white-glove tier stays at ~70% margin including support hours.

06 — Diligence

Every number on this page survived contact with a source.

Four parallel research workstreams re-verified licensing terms, model pricing, vendor costs and RAG economics against live July 2026 sources before this plan was written. Four findings changed it materially.

Finding 01 · Licensing

The obvious build is illegal.

The most-recommended off-the-shelf stack (Open WebUI + Dify) prohibits white-label, multi-tenant SaaS without unpublished-price commercial licences — and a popular workflow engine wants ~$50k/year to embed. Competitors assembling these tools carry a forced-rebuild risk they haven't priced.

→ Chosen stack is MIT / Apache end-to-end. Zero licensing overhang.
Finding 02 · Pricing

The "free" model wasn't. The cheap one had expired.

A workhorse model billed as free actually costs $0.06 / $0.40 per M tokens; a quoted premium-model price turned out to be promotional pricing that expired 31 May 2026 — real list is 4× higher. This market reprices monthly.

→ Economics rebuilt on list prices, padded 2×. Router re-verified monthly.
Finding 03 · Privacy

The privacy premium doesn't exist.

The same open-weight models served from US zero-data-retention infrastructure cost 0–33% less than their Chinese home APIs. The trade-off everyone assumes between cost and data protection largely isn't there — if you pick the right host.

→ Privacy-safe routing is the default, at no margin cost. A sales weapon with SMBs.
Finding 04 · Margins

The 99.5% margin claim was fiction.

A prior draft's "$30/month total cost at 50 tenants" omitted meeting bots (~$0.54/hr), email APIs ($2/account), search, parsing and support. True COGS is ~80–100× that figure — and the model still holds ~84% blended margin.

→ A plan that survives an investor's spreadsheet, because it already survived ours.

Standing cadence: model prices, vendor terms and licence texts are re-verified monthly. The routing matrix is configuration — corrections ship without touching product code.

08 — Risk Register

What could go wrong — and what's already in place.

R1Model & price churn — monthly in this market

The router makes models configuration, not code. Prices re-verified on a monthly cadence; unit economics already padded 2× against repricing and usage spikes.

R2Runaway usage costs

Per-tenant budgets enforced at the gateway, pooled caps on meeting hours and images, and graceful degradation to cheaper models instead of surprise invoices — protecting both margin and trust.

R3Trust with sensitive financial data

Database-enforced tenant isolation (row-level security), zero-data-retention hosts by default, draft-first approval on every outbound email, per-tenant audit logs, GDPR export and deletion from launch.

R4Open-source licence exposure

An MIT/Apache-only component policy, verified per component before adoption — the trap that invalidates the naive competitor build is structurally avoided.

R5Managed-tier support burden

Onboarding runbooks, a quarterly (not ad-hoc) review cadence, and a £1,500 setup fee that prices the labour in. If hours exceed the model, the fee moves — not the margin.
